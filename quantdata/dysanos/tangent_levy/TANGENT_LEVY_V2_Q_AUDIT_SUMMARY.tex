\begin{abstract}
This note records the final v2 audit of a reduced Tangent L\'evy construction under
the risk-neutral measure $Q$.  The experiment uses the native Tangent L\'evy
short-end spot compensator, a deterministic Musiela shift, and a restricted
universe consisting of spot plus 44 fixed-cash-strike options.  The two
conditional drift tests do not reject the $Q$ martingale restriction.  The
literal coordinate-wise unconditional Gaussian diagnostic reports many extreme
statistics, but those statistics are dominated by compound-Poisson coordinates
whose compensating jumps are too rare to be sampled by 100,000 paths.  The
result is therefore evidence about this reduced $Q$ construction, not a
successful calibration of the full stochastic model in the paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and implementation}

The population contains 100,000 simulated paths (50,000 antithetic pairs),
initialized from the 1 June 2022 SPX surface.  The tested trading universe is
spot plus 44 options: eleven normalized-moneyness instruments at each of 5, 10,
15, and 20 business-day maturities.  Strikes remain fixed in cash units over
each one-step return.  Spot is advanced with the Tangent L\'evy codebook's
short-end compensator, while the codebook is transported by deterministic
Musiela shift substeps.

The implementation includes the finite-activity no-jump atom in transform
pricing, lattice-consistent FFT pricing, removal of numerical FFT dust, tail
cutoffs under both cash and share intensities, short-end midpoint evaluation in
Musiela substeps, float64 audit returns, and exact fold-level entropy scoring.
These details are necessary for the simulated spot and option prices to be
internally consistent under $Q$.

\section{Calibration limitation}

The attempted historical reconstruction did not pass its mandatory gate on any
of 335 dates.  Across the history the maximum relative $L^1$ error was 1.3817
and the maximum repricing RMSE was 525 quoted half-spreads.  For the retained
1 June 2022 initialization, the relative $L^1$ error is 0.9473, the repricing
RMSE is 20.185 half-spreads, and the maximum absolute normalized price error is
0.00977.  This confirms the expected poor fit.

Because the reconstructed history supplied no positive covariance mode, the
final audit uses zero stochastic factors.  It is a deterministic-codebook
Tangent L\'evy model, not a fitted version of the paper's full stochastic
factor/HJM specification.  The drift-test methodology is adapted from
\texttt{paper/main2.tex}; that paper's underlying SDLV experiment uses a
Gaussian/information-bridge spot construction and does not by itself calibrate
the sampling distribution of a rare-event compound-Poisson diagnostic.

Both conditional confidence intervals contain zero and both absolute
$t$ statistics are below 1.96.  Accordingly, neither conditional test rejects
the null of zero predictable drift at the 5\% two-sided level.

The literal unconditional report tests 570 time--instrument coordinates.  It
finds a maximum absolute $z$ statistic of $1.485\times10^9$, a 99th percentile
of $1.196\times10^8$, 204 coordinates above 1.96, 150 above 2.576, and 138
above 5.  Read literally as Gaussian tests, these coordinates fail.

That literal reading is not statistically valid for the problematic
coordinates.  Some reconstructed annual intensities are approximately
$1.5\times10^{-6}$, corresponding to a one-day jump probability near
$5.95\times10^{-9}$.  A 100,000-path population is therefore overwhelmingly
likely to contain no compensating jump.  The empirical variance collapses while
the analytically compensated mean remains nonzero, producing enormous
studentized ratios.  On the 432 coordinates with pair standard error exceeding
$10^{-12}$, the maximum absolute statistic is 3.597, the 99th percentile is
3.551, 66 exceed 1.96, 12 exceed 2.576, and none exceeds 5.  This filtered view
is diagnostic only; it is not a replacement preregistered test.

\section{Conclusion}

The strongest defensible conclusion is narrow.  The two requested conditional
tests find no detectable predictable drift under the reduced $Q$ simulation.
The unconditional Gaussian coordinate test cannot confirm the same claim at
this Monte Carlo size because the fitted finite-activity model contains events
far rarer than the population can resolve.  Its extreme statistics are a
rare-event resolution failure, not direct evidence of an exploitable
arbitrage.  A definitive unconditional assessment would require analytic
conditional moments, importance sampling, or a test explicitly designed for
the compound-Poisson law.

This audit does not validate the market fit, the physical-measure dynamics, or
the omitted stochastic codebook factors.  It establishes that the implemented
reduced construction uses the Tangent L\'evy compensator consistently and that
its two cross-fitted conditional drift tests pass on the retained population.
