\begin{abstract}
This note records a deliberately misspecified Tangent L\'evy experiment
initialized on the same 29 August 2025 SPX session used by the SDLV study in
\texttt{main2.tex}.  The double-exponential static family fits the market
poorly and no retained historical reconstruction passes the production gate.
Nevertheless, a target-local 16-factor diffusion is estimated from historical
codebook changes and evolved under the convolutional Tangent L\'evy HJM drift.
The requested unconditional and strategy-conditional $Q$ diagnostics are then
applied to 100,000 simulated paths in the paper's restricted universe of spot
plus 44 fixed-cash-strike options.  The no-arbitrage conclusion is reported
separately from, and does not rehabilitate, the failed market fit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and initialization}

The initial state is calibrated to the SPX option book dated 29 August 2025.
The book contains 4,036 retained call-equivalent bid/ask observations over 32
expiries.  Historical codebooks span 3 January 2022 through 31 December 2024.
Of 729 requested sessions, 726 are retained and 698 adjacent transitions are
valid one-session covariance observations.  Three sessions are excluded by
the unchanged quote-book integrity gate: 10 May 2023, 17 November 2023, and
11 December 2023.  No crossed or otherwise invalid observation is repaired.

The target spot and all option values are generated consistently from the
Tangent L\'evy codebook.  Spot follows the compensated compound-Poisson law
implied by the current short-end L\'evy density.  Options are repriced from the
simulated forward density with a lattice-consistent compound-Poisson FFT.

\section{Static calibration result}

The independent per-expiry optimization converges, but the family has poor
capacity for this surface.  Only 1,375 of 4,036 observations (34.07\%) lie
inside bid/ask.  The excess-price RMSE is 5.202 quoted half-spreads.  Enforcing
calendar consistency leaves 898 observations (22.25\%) inside bid/ask and an
RMSE of 6.704 half-spreads.  Reconstructing the 71-node daily forward-density
grid leaves 224 observations (5.55\%) inside bid/ask and an RMSE of 25.427
half-spreads.

The target reconstruction requires relative $L^1$ modification 1.0498 and
has a repricing RMSE of 6.938 half-spreads, so it is a \fail{} against the
pre-registered reconstruction gate.  All 726 historical states fail the same
gate.  This is the expected model-capacity result, not an optimizer failure.

\section{Historical diffusion and measures}

Daily forward-density changes are centered and projected into the discrete
mass and exponential-moment constraint nullspace.  Admissible coordinates are
selected relative to the 29 August 2025 initial density, while covariance is
estimated solely from the 698 historical one-session transitions.  Sixteen
factors retain 95.002\% of projected variance over 142 active coordinates out
of 21,371.  The maximum factor-constraint residual is
$3.23\times10^{-17}$.

Under $Q$, the volatility fields determine the convolutional HJM drift.  A
separate constant market-price-of-risk regression estimates the descriptive
$P$ drift in the same factor span; it explains 99.995\% of the fitted target
drift with residual RMSE 210.206 in codebook-drift units.  This $P$ regression
is not used in either no-arbitrage test.

The Gaussian factor system otherwise reaches the positivity boundary almost
immediately.  Simulation therefore applies a predictable global scalar time
change: factor loadings are multiplied by $\gamma$, their quadratic HJM drift
by $\gamma^2$, and the $P$ risk-premium term (when used) by $\gamma$.  The
scale is fixed before each shock and reserves twelve conditional standard
deviations of coordinate-wise headroom.  Density clipping, shock resampling,
and path deletion are forbidden.  The final $\gamma$ diagnostics are essential
to interpreting the drift tests.

\section{Risk-neutral audit design}

The audit follows \texttt{main2.tex}: 100,000 paths form 50,000 independent
antithetic pairs; each path covers 20 business days with eight substeps per
day.  The trading universe is spot and eleven initially out-of-the-money
fixed-cash-strike options at each of 5, 10, 15, and 20 business-day expiries.
For each live contract the tested one-day return is the value change divided
by pre-step spot.

The unconditional diagnostic first averages every antithetic pair and then
forms a studentized mean for each live day--instrument coordinate.  The two
conditional diagnostics fit uncapped affine-plus-width-32 SiLU policies on one
pair-preserving fold and evaluate their predictable portfolio return on the
opposite fold.  One uses the squared objective and the other the entropy
objective specified in \texttt{main2.tex}.  A conditional test passes at the
5\% two-sided level when its out-of-fold $95\%$ interval contains zero.

\section{Results}

All 100,000 paths complete without clipping, deletion, or a frozen path.  The
time change is nevertheless dominant: its minimum is
$2.13\times10^{-10}$ and its mean is 0.05452.  Of 16,000,000 path--substeps,
15,858,939 (99.12\%) are boundary-limited, 14,182,352 have $\gamma<0.1$,
12,516,601 have $\gamma<0.01$, and 9,211,196 have
$\gamma<10^{-4}$.

The unconditional screen is a clear \fail{}.  Among all 570 live coordinates,
the maximum absolute $z$ statistic is 40.632 and the 99th percentile is
28.089.  There are 276 coordinates above 1.96, 229 above 2.576, and 134 above
5.  These counts are far beyond the ordinary multiple-testing frequency under
a standard-normal coordinate null.

\section{Interpretation}

The stochastic 29 August 2025 construction does \emph{not} pass the requested
$Q$ no-arbitrage diagnostics.  This is not evidence that Tangent L\'evy theory
is arbitrageable.  It is evidence that the present empirical/discretized
construction---a very poor static reconstruction, historical factors defined
on only 142 target-admissible coordinates, and an almost always active global
boundary time change---does not preserve the fixed-contract martingale
identities closely enough for the 100,000-path test.

The predictable time change preserves the continuous-time HJM scaling
algebraically, but it does not cure finite-step transport/pricing bias, and its
near-freezing makes the fitted historical diffusion economically remote from
the unscaled estimate.  Accordingly, neither the failed drift test nor any
future non-rejection should be generalized beyond this restricted universe and
strategy class.  The correct empirical conclusion is that this stochastic
specification is unsuitable as a risk-neutral model in its current form.
