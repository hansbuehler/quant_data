Tables~\ref{tab:surface-interpolation-literature},
\ref{tab:surface-representation-literature},
\ref{tab:inverse-calibration-literature}, and
\ref{tab:surface-dynamics-literature} separate four questions which are often
conflated: constructing one admissible surface, compressing or generating a
market state, solving an underlying-model inverse problem, and learning a
surface's evolution through time.  We use common classification
symbols throughout the tables.\footnote{\textbf{S$\infty$}: a theorem- or
architecture-level guarantee on the full continuous surface; \textbf{S$\Omega$}:
the same guarantee only on a stated bounded fitting domain; \textbf{SG}: a
strict guarantee only on the modelled finite grid; \textbf{A}: approximate,
using penalties, reweighting, rejection, or empirical violation rates;
\textbf{N}: no static-arbitrage guarantee; \textbf{U}: arbitrage freedom is
claimed, but the available construction does not suffice to verify its
continuum scope.}
Constraints imposed inside a fit are distinguished from a projection or
iterative repair applied after an unconstrained fit; only the former makes the
fitted family itself constrained.  Option counts refer to native surface
coordinates rather than option-day observations.  An off-grid count of
$\infty$ means that the representation can be evaluated on a continuum, subject
to its stated domain and tail assumptions.
