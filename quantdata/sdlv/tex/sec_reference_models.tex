\section{Reference-model implementation details}
\label{app:reference-models}

\subsection{Tangent Levy}
\label{app:tangent-levy}

Our Tangent Levy benchmark follows the codebook construction of
\citet{TangentLevy}, but is an in-repository implementation rather than the
authors' software.  It is paper-aligned rather than a literal reproduction.
At each observed expiry we first fit a double-exponential jump density to
forward-normalized call-equivalent bid--ask intervals.  For each jump node, the
forward L\'evy density is then represented by 12 non-negative cubic B-spline
coefficients in maturity.  Analytic spline integrals map these coefficients to
the fitted average L\'evy measures; non-negative least squares with a curvature
penalty supplies a smooth positive reconstruction.  We evaluate it on a daily
maturity grid from zero to 70 business days and a 301-node jump grid on
$[-0.75,0.75]$.

The historical diffusion fit uses every other daily maturity and the seven
nonzero jump nodes with highest average training intensity.  Linear
prolongation maps this reduced grid to the full codebook, and the reduced
factors are projected onto the discrete exponential-moment constraint before
PCA.  The leading 16 directions are estimated from 698 consecutive-session
changes ending on 31 December 2024, explain 95.23\% of reduced-grid variation,
and use 5\% covariance shrinkage.  Simulation integrates the full-grid
loadings into eight substep cells per business day, applies the exact cell
shift, computes the quadratic convolutional HJM drift on its complete support,
and drives spot with the compensated shortest-maturity jump law.  The maximum
fitted moment-constraint residual is $5.13\times10^{-16}$.

To retain non-negative finite-step densities, each path uses a predictable
scalar time change $\gamma$: factor loadings are multiplied by $\gamma$ and
the quadratic HJM drift by $\gamma^2$.  The scale is fixed before observing the
Gaussian increment by solving the exact quadratic positivity bound with
12 conditional-standard-deviation headroom.  This preserves the HJM identity
but can materially slow the fitted dynamics near the boundary.  In the
100,000-path audit the mean and minimum scales are 0.133 and 0.0264; no substep
has $\gamma<0.01$ and no path freezes.

The initial-fit results in Figures~\ref{fig:initial-fit-iv}
and~\ref{fig:initial-fit-spread} use the final daily-grid reconstruction
supplied to the simulator.  This distinction is important: independent
maturity fits are not a dynamically admissible initial codebook.  The
reconstruction on 29 August 2025 did not pass its pre-specified repricing gate,
so the poor fit shown there is a reported numerical limitation, not suppressed
calibration error.  The
subsequent risk-neutral diagnostics therefore test the implemented projected
Q dynamics conditional on this retained initial state; they do not turn the
failed static reconstruction into a successful market calibration.

The restricted-universe audit uses 100,000 paths arranged as 50,000
antithetic pairs over 20 business days.  Across its 570 live
day--instrument coordinates, the maximum and 99th-percentile absolute
$z$-scores are 2.191 and 2.126, with no coordinate above 2.576.  The two-fold
out-of-fold conditional statistics are $T_{\mathrm{sqr}}=-0.768$ and
$T_{\mathrm{ent}}=-0.575$; both 95\% confidence intervals contain zero.

\subsection{Bates CRC}
\label{app:bates-crc}

Our CRC benchmark is an in-repository implementation of the generalized
Bates construction and Algorithm~1 of
\citet{DysLitGambaraTeichmann2021}, not the authors' software.  The state has
Heston stochastic variance and compensated compound-Poisson Gaussian log
jumps.  Ten increasing target maturities are grouped into five adjacent
pairs; each pair has its own jump mean and standard deviation, while the
Poisson intensity and Heston mean-reversion speed are held fixed across
recalibrations.  Carr--Madan inversion with damping parameter $1.5$ supplies
the option prices.  We retain a scalar direct solve as an inverse-map oracle
and subject the learned map to independent exact-pricer reset gates.  The path
simulation also records its fixed-option price discontinuities, so a failed
numerical inversion is not silently reported as an exact CRC reset.

All historical inputs end strictly before 29 August 2025.  From the 890
earlier sessions we estimate $\kappa=15.0$ and jump intensity
$\lambda=4.25197$ per year.  The target-date calibration then fits $V_0$,
$\theta$, $\xi$, $\rho$, and the five jump mean/standard-deviation pairs to
the same 2,662 quotes used in Figure~\ref{fig:initial-fit-iv}.  It converges to
$V_0=0.007474$, $\theta=0.0100$, $\xi=0.5000$, and $\rho=-0.9900$; the latter
three are on their calibration bounds, consistent with the limited static
fit shown in Figures~\ref{fig:initial-fit-iv}
and~\ref{fig:initial-fit-spread}.

The exogenous reset law is estimated from 764 daily proxy states from 7 July
2022 through 28 August 2025.  Each row uses a trailing 63-session window ending
on that date: $\theta$ is the median 20-business-day ATM implied variance,
$\xi$ is the RMS CIR-normalized variance innovation, and $\rho$ is the
spot/variance-innovation correlation.  We fit a Gaussian law to
$(\Delta\log\theta,\Delta\log\xi,\Delta\operatorname{atanh}\rho)$, cap each
relative update at 5\%, keep $\kappa$ fixed, and impose the Feller condition
after every update.  These are backward-only dynamic proxies, not daily
full-surface Bates calibrations.

We train the two inverse-recalibration maps on 600,000 synthetic
generalized-Bates surfaces generated on the target calibration's exact ten
maturities and 13 relative-strike nodes, using 240-point Gauss--Legendre
Fourier integration on $[0,600]$; 540,000 surfaces form the training split and
60,000 whole-shard observations are held out.  Of the 78 million generated
surface nodes, 193,840 (0.249\%) required clipping back inside the Black price
bounds; the generator rejects a surface if that correction exceeds
$5\times10^{-4}$ in normalized price.  NN1 maps the fourteen
model coordinates $(V,\theta,\xi,\rho,\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_5,
\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_5)$ to the 130 raw Black volatilities.  NN2 receives
the updated $(\theta,\xi,\rho)$ and the protected 130-node surface and returns
the ten jump coordinates through a sigmoid stretched to their training
bounds: $[-0.30,0.15]$ for each mean and $[0.005,0.30]$ for each standard
deviation.  Both maps are plain four-hidden-layer, width-1,024 ELU networks.  We
first train NN1 with raw implied-volatility MSE and then freeze it while
training NN2 through NN1, using batches of 1,000.  This preserves the
input--output construction of Algorithm~1, but does not reproduce the
authors' batch-normalized residual cell and is another reason we describe the
benchmark as paper-aligned rather than a literal software reproduction.

The restricted audit uses the same geometry as the other reference models:
100,000 paths arranged as 50,000 adjacent antithetic pairs, 20 business days,
eight substeps per day, and the fixed spot-plus-44-option universe.  Between
daily resets we use full-truncation Heston variance and compensated Gaussian
jumps from the shortest live jump bucket.  At each day boundary the old
codebook is shifted by one day, its ten-by-13 continuation surface is repriced,
the backward-estimated parameter innovation is applied, and NN2 reconstructs
the new five jump means and standard deviations.  The implementation records
the resulting fixed-option price discontinuity explicitly before any
no-arbitrage statistic is accepted.

That gate fails for the retained NN2 checkpoint: none of
\CrcResetGateSamples\ independently drawn reset states meets the
$\CrcResetGateTolerance$ spot-normalized exact-pricing tolerance.  The median
maximum error across its calibration and inter-node held-out options is
$\CrcResetGateMedianError$ and the maximum is $\CrcResetGateMaximumError$.
We nevertheless retain the \CrcQPathCount-path restricted audit as a negative
control for the diagnostics.  It has maximum and 99th-percentile coordinate
$z$-scores \CrcQMaximumAbsZ\ and \CrcQP99AbsZ; \CrcQAboveTwoFiveSevenSix\ of
\CrcQCoordinates\ coordinates exceed 2.576 and \CrcQAboveFive\ exceed five.
The two-fold out-of-fold conditional statistics are
$T_{\mathrm{sqr}}=\CrcQSquaredT$ and $T_{\mathrm{ent}}=\CrcQEntropyT$.
These rejections detect the reset-repricing defect; they do not establish that
an exact consistent-recalibration model is arbitrageable.

\subsection{Matched initial-fit convention}

For the comparison we use the exact 2,662 quotes retained by the GROMM
25-expiry calibration on 29 August 2025.  GROMM is the unrestricted
$25\times200$ state from the
\texttt{direct-continuation-\allowbreak1000-v1} run after
1,000 end-to-end epochs with a 5\% annual Black backbone; this is the same
checkpoint as the direct fit in Table~\ref{tab:numerics-static-market-fit}.
Tangent Levy is repriced at precisely those strikes and expiries from its
daily-grid density, and CRC from its retained generalized-Bates target fit.
Put observations are converted to normalized
call-equivalent prices by parity before Black-volatility inversion.  If $b$,
$a$, and $h=(a-b)/2$ are the bid, ask, and half-spread, respectively, the
signed price surface reports
\[
  \widetilde e=
  \begin{cases}
    (C-a)/h,&C>a,\\
    0,&b\leq C\leq a,\\
    (C-b)/h,&C<b,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad e=|\widetilde e|.
\]
The volatility surface applies the same construction after transforming the
bid and ask to Black volatility.  Thus ``quotes with excess'' is the fraction
with $e>0$, and the conditional mean and median are computed only over those
failed quotes.  All three models use the same observations, spreads, axes, and
colour scales across all three panels.
